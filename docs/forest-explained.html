The Forest

How a vocabulary becomes a wood — the growth mechanism, the six stages, and the technologies that draw them
Written for: someone who needs to explain or extend this feature
Scope: app/services/forest.py, app/services/fsrs.py, features/forest/

System: FluencyOS  ·  Electron + React client, FastAPI + SQLite server
Scheduler: FSRS 4.5, implemented in-repo, published default weights
Scene: three.js, procedural low-poly geometry with optional GLB packs

1. What the forest is

One tree per saved word. The tree's size is how well the word is remembered, its colour is how much trouble it is in, and none of it is stored. There is no trees table, no growth counter, no per-tree row anywhere in the database.

Everything on the screen is computed, on request, from tables the rest of the app already maintains: vocab_words for what was saved, review_cards for how well it is held, and review_logs for how it was used. The forest is a drawing of the scheduler's state, not a second copy of it.

That is a deliberate design choice with a practical consequence. A stored tree would drift: the forest would show a thriving oak for a word the scheduler knows was forgotten last week. Because nothing is stored, the two can never disagree, and the forest cannot be gamed. It grows when the learner's memory grows, and by no other means.

The one exception. Focus sessions are written down (forest_focus_sessions), because sitting through twenty-five minutes is a decision, not something derivable from other tables.

What the screen shows

  • the planted clearing itself, in 3D, one plant per word;
  • Growth — how many words stand at each of the six stages;
  • Tallest tree — the best-held word in the collection;
  • Focus session — a timer you commit to and mark as sat through;
  • a detail card for whichever plant is hovered or clicked: its stage, how long it holds for, its health, its lapses and how often it has been said unprompted.

The three routes

GET /forest?user_id=… the whole forest, in one query POST /forest/focus start a focus session (1–180 minutes) POST /forest/focus/{id}/complete mark it sat through, checked against the clock

The scene is loaded lazily behind a React Suspense boundary, so three.js and the geometry are not part of the bundle the app opens with — the forest pays for itself only when it is visited.

Why FSRS is implemented here rather than installed

The published fsrs package would be one more thing to fetch at launch. The app starts its backend with uv run, which re-syncs dependencies on every start, so a package that cannot be downloaded does not degrade the scheduler — it stops the whole application from starting. The algorithm is about a hundred lines of published arithmetic with no dependencies of its own, so it lives in the repository.

3. The growth mechanism

A tree's size is one number: the word's stability. FSRS defines stability as the number of days until the probability of recalling this word falls to 90%. A word with a stability of 3 is one you would probably still know in three days; a word with a stability of 90 is one you would probably still know in three months.

That number is already a measure of how well-rooted a word is, so the six stages are simply readable names for bands of it.

One word, from saved to planted

The six bands

The thresholds live in one line of forest.py: STAGE_DAYS = (1, 3, 7, 21, 60). They climb roughly threefold because stability itself grows that way — the distance from Seed to Sprout is a day, and from Young tree to Ancient is two months.

StageStabilityWhat it means about the word
Seednever reviewed,
or < 1 day		Saved, but nothing yet shows you can hold it. Every word starts here.
Sprout1 – 3 daysHeld overnight. One successful review, or a shaky one.
Seedling3 – 7 daysHeld for most of a week.
Sapling7 – 21 daysHeld for a week or more. Past the point where most forgetting happens.
Young tree21 – 60 daysHeld for a month. This word is largely yours.
Ancient tree60 days +Held for two months or more, and climbing by years per review.
A new card is always a Seed, whatever stability it claims. stage_for checks state == "new" first, so a word that has never been answered cannot appear as anything else.

What moves a word up

Only a rating. The first one sets the stability outright, from the FSRS default weights:

First answerStability it setsStage it lands in
Again0.49 daysSeed
Hard1.40 daysSprout
Good3.71 daysSeedling
Easy13.82 daysSapling

After that, each successful recall multiplies stability by a growth factor that depends on how hard the card is, how stable it already was, and how close to forgetting it you were when you answered. Answering a card late, when recall had genuinely decayed, grows it more than answering it early.

Run with the real scheduler, always answering Good exactly when the card falls due:

review 1: stability 3.7 d next in 4 d -> Seedling review 2: stability 22.7 d next in 23 d -> Young tree review 3: stability 108.6 d next in 109 d -> Ancient tree

So an Ancient tree takes only three correct answers — but it takes roughly four weeks of calendar time to give them, because each one has to wait for the card to come due. This is the mechanism that makes the forest honest: you cannot sit down for an hour and grow one.

What brings a word down

Forgetting. A lapse does not nudge stability, it collapses it, from the same real scheduler:

40.0 d -> 4.3 d Young tree -> Seedling

The tree shrinks back to where the memory actually is. It is the same number the scheduler will use to decide when you next see the card, so the picture and the queue always agree.

Ratings come from two places. A flashcard answer is one. Using the word in conversation is the other — spoken outcomes map onto the same four ratings and move the same FSRS state. A word you say unprompted grows its tree exactly as a card you answered would.

What each number is telling you

Seed: 25. These are words you have saved but not yet answered — or answered and got wrong. They are not weak memories; for the most part they are memories that have not been tested at all. This is the normal shape for a collection that is being filled faster than it is being reviewed, which is what reading with lookups does. A single review session moves most of them.

Sprout: 11. Answered once, holding for a day or two. This is the most fragile band — a word here is genuinely at risk, and the scheduler will bring it back within days.

Seedling 2, Sapling 3, Young tree 2. Seven words have survived several reviews spread over weeks. The dip at Seedling is not a problem: these bands are narrow (3–7 days, then 7–21) and a word passes through them quickly when reviews are answered on time.

Ancient tree: 0. Expected, and not a failure. Sixty days of stability cannot be reached in less than about a month of elapsed time whatever you do, because each review has to wait for its due date.

The shape to aim for is a pyramid that slowly inverts. Early on, almost everything is a Seed. As reviews accumulate, the mass moves right. If the Seed count keeps climbing while the right-hand bands stay flat, the collection is growing faster than it is being reviewed — the fix is review sessions, not more saving.

Why the tallest tree matters

The panel names the best-held word in the collection. It is the one number in the forest that can only go up honestly: it is the highest stability any word has reached, and nothing but repeated, correctly-spaced recall produces it.

5. Health, wilt and dormancy

Size says how well a word is held. Colour says whether it is in trouble. The two are computed separately and deliberately do not mix.

health = max(0, 100 - lapses × 12) # permanent scars if overdue: wilt = min(1, overdue_days / 14) # current trouble health = health × (1 - wilt)

Lapses are scars: a word forgotten three times carries that history permanently, at twelve points each. Being overdue is a wilt: it deepens for a fortnight and it lifts the moment the word is reviewed. They are kept apart because one number covering both would say the same thing about a word forgotten three times and relearned — which is fine now — and a word never forgotten but three weeks overdue, which is not.

StateCanopyMeaning
Healthygreen #63b355No lapses, not overdue.
Wiltingtowards amber #c2a85aLapsed before, or overdue now. Still recoverable.
Dormantgrey #9a9a8eSuspended, or more than 30 days overdue.

A dormant plant has dropped its leaves rather than died: reviewing the word wakes it, at whatever stability it still holds. Its stage does not change while it sleeps — dormancy is about attention, not about memory.

6. How the scene draws it

The 3D clearing is built from the same numbers, with nothing added and nothing stored.

PropertyComes fromRule
Which planta hash of the wordBroadleaf, conifer or shrub. The same word is always the same plant, so the forest is recognisable between visits.
How bigthe stageScale runs 0.42 → 1.9 across the six stages.
Canopy colourhealth and dormancyGreen through amber; grey when dormant.
Accent coloura hash of the wordPetals and caps keep their own colour whatever the health, or the plot stops reading as a garden.
Where it standsblue-noise samplingFor each plant, twelve candidate spots are thrown and the one furthest from everything already planted wins.
How far apartthe mean stageSpacing grows with the size of what is planted, so a young vocabulary reads as undergrowth and a mature one as woodland.

Two traps this avoids, both tried first: a plain grid reads as an orchard, and jittering a grid only produces a wonky orchard because the eye finds the rows anyway. Pure random placement is the other extreme — it clumps, and trees end up standing inside each other.

The geometry is procedural: cones and a tapered cylinder with flat shading, which is how low-poly foliage is made, so the scene is complete with nothing downloaded. A converted GLB model pack drops in alongside if one is present.

7. Focus sessions

A focus session is a block of time — between 1 and 180 minutes — that the learner commits to. It is the only part of the forest that is written to the database, because it is the only part that is a decision rather than a derivation.

Completion is checked against the clock, not taken from the client: a timer marked complete by a button press is a button, not a commitment. It pays nothing. The record that it happened is the whole of it.

Previously: focus minutes and review outcomes earned sunlight, a currency spent on streak freezes and on reviving dormant trees. It has been removed. The streak freeze was the reason — it could be bought, it was counted on screen, and nothing in the streak calculation ever consulted it, so the streak it promised to protect broke anyway. The forest now rewards nothing and reflects everything.

The one sentence to remember

The forest has no state of its own. Every tree is a rendering of one number the scheduler owns — how many days that word will survive in memory — so the wood can only grow when the memory does.
